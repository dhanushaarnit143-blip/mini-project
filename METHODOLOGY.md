*Investigational Research Prototype — Scientific Methodology & Evaluation Protocol*

## 1. Research Design & Clinical Framing

### 1.1 The Prodromal Screening Problem
Idiopathic Parkinson's disease (PD) exhibits a prolonged prodromal phase lasting 5 to 20 years before the onset of classic cardinal motor signs (bradykinesia, rigidity, rest tremor). By the time a patient presents with motor impairment sufficient for clinical diagnosis, an estimated **50–70% of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta have already degenerated**.

Interventions aiming at neuroprotection or disease modification must be initiated during this prodromal window. However, identifying at-risk individuals in the general population presents two fundamental challenges:
1. **Low Positive Predictive Value of Single Markers:** Individual prodromal markers (such as isolated hyposmia or subtle voice jitter) have low specificity when screened in isolation.
2. **Missing Modality Reality:** In primary care and community health clinics, participants rarely undergo all specialized evaluations (e.g. retinal fundus photography, computerized gait labs, and acoustic recording) at a single visit.

### 1.2 The MPF-PD Hypothesized Solution
The MPF-PD methodology investigates whether a **multimodal gated neural fusion architecture** can:
- Integrate heterogeneous non-invasive markers (olfactory, sleep, voice, gait, retinal microvasculature).
- Learn non-linear cross-modality feature interactions.
- Dynamically adapt its internal attention weights to handle partial observations when one or more modalities are absent.
- Provide interpretable, mathematically sound risk attributions via exact Shapley values.

## 2. Participant-Level Partitioning & Leakage Prevention

Data leakage is the most frequent cause of overoptimistic performance in medical machine learning. In the MPF-PD methodology, six strict leakage barriers are enforced programmatically.

```
Total Cohort (N = 350 Participants)
  │
  ├─ Stratified Group Split by Participant ID (seed = 42)
  │
  ├── Training Partition:    70% (N = 245 Participants, 122 Control, 123 Case)
  │     └─ Preprocessing Fit (Imputers, Scalers, Encoders fitted ONLY here)
  │
  ├── Validation Partition:  15% (N = 52 Participants, 25 Control, 27 Case)
  │     └─ Hyperparameter Tuning & Early Stopping (Transformed via Train stats)
  │
  └── Held-Out Test Split:   15% (N = 53 Participants, 26 Control, 27 Case)
        └─ Final Benchmark & Evaluation (Transformed via Train stats, ZERO refit)
```

### 2.1 The Six Anti-Leakage Rules
1. **Strict Participant Disjointness:** Partitions are generated by unique participant identifier (`participant_id`). A participant appearing in the training split cannot appear in validation or testing under any circumstance.
2. **Recording-Level Isolation:** For time-series or multi-trial modalities (e.g., multiple sustained vowel recordings or multiple 2-minute gait walking bouts from the same subject), all recordings from a participant are clustered together within the participant's assigned partition.
3. **Preprocessor State Encapsulation:** Imputers (`SimpleImputer`), normalizers, and scalers (`StandardScaler`) are fitted strictly on `df_train`. Validation and test splits are transformed using the saved training parameters. No refitting or joint fit-transform is permitted.
4. **Feature Selection Independence:** Feature sets are fixed *a priori* based on clinical consensus literature (e.g. Movement Disorder Society prodromal criteria). Supervised feature selection using test or validation labels is strictly prohibited.
5. **Absence of Target / Temporal Leakage:** Feature matrices contain only baseline cross-sectional data. No post-baseline variables, downstream clinical outcomes, or longitudinal follow-up data are included in feature vectors.
6. **Programmatic Audit Verification:** Before any evaluation metrics are logged, an automated leakage detector (`evaluation/leakage_report.json`) verifies zero set intersection between $\text{Train}_{\text{IDs}}$, $\text{Val}_{\text{IDs}}$, and $\text{Test}_{\text{IDs}}$.

## 3. Data Validation & Physiological Plausibility Bounding

Incoming raw data streams pass through a strict Pydantic validation and physiological bounds checker:

| Feature / Modality | Allowed Data Type | Physiological Lower Bound | Physiological Upper Bound | Out-of-Bounds Policy |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| `participant_age` | Float | 18.0 years | 110.0 years | Impute median (65.0) + log warning |
| `upsit_total_score` | Integer / Float | 0 | 40 | Clamp to $[0, 40]$ + flag anomaly |
| `rbdsq_total` | Integer / Float | 0 | 13 | Clamp to $[0, 13]$ + flag anomaly |
| `voice_jitter_pct` | Float | 0.0 % | 20.0 % | Clamp to $[0.0, 20.0]$ |
| `voice_hnr_db` | Float | -5.0 dB | 50.0 dB | Clamp to $[-5.0, 50.0]$ |
| `gait_speed_m_per_s`| Float | 0.1 m/s | 3.0 m/s | Clamp to $[0.1, 3.0]$ |
| `vessel_density` | Float | 0.001 | 0.500 | Clamp to $[0.001, 0.500]$ |

If an input exceeds valid bounds, the system does not crash; it logs a structured warning, applies boundary clamping or median imputation, and flags the record with an uncertainty note.

## 4. Model Selection & Training Strategy

### 4.1 Modality-Specific Encoders
- **Objective:** Train lightweight supervised baselines and deep feature encoders capable of projecting high-dimensional or heterogeneous inputs into a regularized 32-dimensional embedding space.
- **Cross-Validation:** 5-fold stratified cross-validation on the training set to optimize hyperparameter regularization (L2 penalties, tree depth).
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer ($\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, weight decay $= 10^{-4}$) with cosine annealing schedule.

### 4.2 Gated Multimodal Fusion
- **Objective:** Learn dynamic attention weights that emphasize reliable present modalities while completely filtering out absent modalities.
- **Loss Function:** Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}$) combined with an L1 regularization penalty on the attention gate weights to encourage sparsity:
  $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(y, \hat{y}) + \lambda \sum_{m=1}^5 \alpha_m^2$$
- **Missing Modality Simulation During Training:** To make the attention network robust to missing inputs, random dropout of entire modalities ($p_{\text{modality\_drop}} = 0.20$) is applied during training epochs.

### 4.3 Final Classifier Selection
XGBoost was chosen as the final classification engine because:
1. It forms non-linear decision boundaries on low-to-medium dimensional tabular representations ($D=45$) with high data efficiency.
2. It natively supports missing feature handling through default split directions.
3. It allows exact, polynomial-time Shapley value computation via TreeSHAP without Monte Carlo approximations.

## 5. Quantitative Evaluation Protocol

### 5.1 Primary Evaluation Metrics
All models are evaluated on the held-out test split using the following metrics:
- **Discrimination:** Receiver Operating Characteristic Area Under the Curve (**ROC-AUC**) and Precision-Recall Area Under the Curve (**PR-AUC**).
- **Classification Performance:** Accuracy, Sensitivity (Recall), Specificity, Positive Predictive Value (Precision), and Macro F1-Score at a standard $0.50$ decision threshold.
- **Probability Calibration:** **Brier Score** ($\frac{1}{N} \sum (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2$) and expected calibration error (ECE).

### 5.2 Statistical Significance & Bootstrap Confidence Intervals
To assess whether multimodal fusion provides statistically meaningful improvements over single-modality models and naive concatenation:
- **Non-Parametric Bootstrap:** 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the test set are drawn with replacement.
- **95% Confidence Intervals:** The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of metric distributions are reported ($[\text{CI}_{\text{lower}}, \text{CI}_{\text{upper}}]$).
- **Pairwise Difference Testing:** Paired $\Delta \text{AUC} = \text{AUC}_{\text{Gated}} - \text{AUC}_{\text{Baseline}}$ is computed across each bootstrap replicate. If the 95% CI of $\Delta \text{AUC}$ includes zero, the null hypothesis of equivalence cannot be rejected.

## 6. Explainability Methodology (TreeSHAP)

To prevent the system from operating as an uninterpretable "black box", explainability is embedded directly into the inference pipeline:

1. **Local Additive Feature Attribution:** For each individual participant, the prediction log-odds are decomposed into the sum of feature contributions $\phi_i$:
   $$f(x) = E[f(X)] + \sum_{i=1}^{45} \phi_i$$
2. **Missingness Attribution:** The binary presence indicators (indices 40–44) carry explicit Shapley values, quantifying how much the risk score shifted simply because a test (e.g. retinal imaging) was omitted.
3. **Modality Redistribution:** By multiplying the gated attention vector's Shapley values by the dynamic gate weights $\alpha_m$, the system calculates an interpretable percentage contribution for each of the five clinical modalities.

## 7. Boundaries of the Research Prototype

To maintain strict scientific integrity, the boundaries of this research prototype are explicitly defined:

| Dimension | Within Prototype Scope | Outside Prototype Scope (Requires Future Research) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scientific Claim** | Algorithmic feasibility of multimodal gated fusion and missing-modality handling. | Clinical diagnostic efficacy, medical device clearance, or real patient screening. |
| **Validation Cohort** | Synthetic multimodal test fixture with clinical parameter distributions. | Prospective multi-center clinical trials with longitudinal motor phenoconversion. |
| **Data Types** | Numerical tabular features, pre-extracted acoustic metrics, and standardized fundus images. | Raw unstructured clinic notes, free-living smartwatch feeds, or genomic sequencing. |
| **Operational Setting**| Research demonstration via local web dashboard and REST API. | Electronic Health Record (EHR) integration, clinical PACS deployment, or telehealth diagnostic usage. |
